# FocusOS v2 — Days off, and a weekly planner that doesn't pile everything onto one day

**Status:** Draft for review
**Routes touched:** `/plan/week` (Steps 2–4), `/dashboard`, `/plan/day`, `/plan/calendar`, `/settings`, plus `lib/weekplan.ts`, `lib/loadindex.ts`, `lib/dailyloop.ts`, `lib/next-action.ts`, `components/workday-session-provider.tsx`
**Companion to:** `14.FocusOS-v2-Weekly-Loop-and-Automatic-Tracking.md` (this doc's whole subject — §5.4's placement pass and the capacity meter it feeds — is that plan's Step 3/4, now shipped and in real use)
**Principle carried over:** additive and non-destructive at the data layer, same as `14`'s own rule — every new field optional, no migration. §1's "every new surface replaces one" rule is relaxed here on purpose: this doc mostly *repairs* one surface (`/plan/week` Step 4's placement) rather than adding a new one, plus one genuinely new, small piece of UI (marking a day off).

**Evidence basis:** every current-state claim in §2 is grounded in direct source reads of this repository at commit `d7b2a55`, with file and line references given, plus the owner's own verbatim report of the bug in real use (quoted in §0). §2.2's worked example is a hand-traced run of the real `placeProposals` algorithm against a plausible real week, not a live-reproduced screenshot — the mechanism is deterministic and the trace is exact, but see §8 for the one open question that would benefit from a live confirmation pass before Phase 2 ships.

## 0. TL;DR

The complaint, verbatim: *"its scheduling all the work in one day only(probably holiday)."*

That's real, and §2.2 shows exactly why: `/plan/week`'s Step 4 (`14.FocusOS-v2-Weekly-Loop-and-Automatic-Tracking.md` §5.4) places every course-bucket chunk, every backlog task, and every stale-paper reading block by asking one question per candidate — *which day currently has the least committed time?* — and answering it fresh for each one. A day with nothing on it yet (because it's a genuine day off, or just hasn't been touched) answers that question first for a long run of consecutive candidates, because its free capacity is so much larger than every other day's that the running total doesn't catch up until most of the week's work has already landed there. The algorithm has no concept of "this day is off-limits," no per-day cap, and no idea that a revision block belongs near the lecture it's revising rather than wherever's emptiest.

This doc does three things:
1. **Adds a way to mark a day off** (§4, §5.1) — a real gap independent of the placement bug: there is currently no way to tell FocusOS "I'm not working Friday" or "Tuesday was a holiday," so every mechanism that reasons about a day (the capacity meter, Workload, Up next, Start day) treats it like any other, and the weekly planner is free to fill it.
2. **Redesigns Step 4's placement pass** (§5.2) so it stops being a single greedy "least-loaded day" sort: revision lands on the course's own lecture day when there is one that week, backlog/leftover work prefers the weekend, every candidate that isn't pinned to a specific day gets a per-day cap so one empty day can't absorb the whole week, and day-off dates are excluded outright rather than merely deprioritized.
3. **Gives reading (and other undated) items a target day** (§5.3) — "read this by Wednesday" — placed with the same lead-time spreading `14`'s checkpoint-prep proposals already use, instead of the current unlabeled 45-minute floating block.

§5.4 answers the "LLM calls or better heuristics" question the owner posed directly: heuristics first, for the same reason `14` §1 gives ("no feature is AI-only") — deterministic, free, and the exact bug being fixed here is a heuristic bug, not a creativity problem. An optional AI-assisted second pass is described as an additive, opt-in Phase 3, not a replacement.

## 1. Goals, non-goals, and what carries over unchanged

**Goals**

- A day can be marked off — in advance ("I'm taking Friday off") or after the fact ("Tuesday was a holiday") — and every surface that reasons about a day respects it: the weekly planner excludes it from placement and from the capacity meter's `free` term, Workload stops expecting work from it, Up next and Start day say so plainly instead of proposing a task.
- Days off are counted somewhere the owner can see the count, not just individually markable and then forgotten (§4, §5.1's "stats" half of the ask).
- Step 4's proposals spread across the week the way a person planning by hand would: revision near its lecture, leftover work on the weekend, nothing piled onto one day just because that day happened to be empty when the algorithm looked at it.
- A reading (or other undated) item can be given a target day, and its placement behaves like checkpoint prep already does — spread across the days leading up to that target, not dropped as one unlabeled block wherever there's room.

**Non-goals**

- No recurring "day off every Friday" pattern. That's `UserSettings.workingWindow.daysOfWeek` already (`14` §4/§5.3) — a *standing* non-work day. This doc is about a *specific date* exception, on top of that pattern, in either direction (a day off inside the working window, or — not modeled here, see §8 — working through what's normally a day off).
- No leave-balance/quota system (a fixed number of days off per term, approvals, accrual). "Store such stats" is read here as *visibility* — a count and a log — not a budget with a limit. §8 flags this as a real product decision if the owner wants the stronger version.
- No new collection. A day off is one additive field on the existing `Day` document, same as `14`'s own `WeekPlan`/`weeklyPlanning` additions.
- No change to `14`'s data model for `WeekPlan.proposedBlocks`/`targets`/`capacity` — this doc changes *how* `placeProposals` (a pure function, `app/(app)/plan/week/page.tsx`) chooses where a candidate lands, not the shape of what gets written.
- Not attempting a general-purpose constraint solver. The redesigned placement in §5.2 is still a deterministic, single-pass, greedy algorithm — just one with the right tie-breaks and exclusions instead of one blind one. §8 asks whether that's enough or whether a real search (try the whole week, backtrack on conflicts) is worth the complexity later.

## 2. What's broken today, with receipts

### 2.1 There is no way to mark a day off

Grep across the whole source tree for `holiday`, `day off`, `dayOff`, `vacation`, `leave` (as a noun) turns up nothing except unrelated uses of the English word "leave" as a verb. The closest existing concept is `Term.kind` (`"semester" | "break" | "none"`, `types/index.ts:314`) via `isBreakMode` (`lib/terms.ts:9-12`) — but a `Term` is a multi-week academic-calendar object (a whole semester or a whole break), created on `/settings` (`14` §8 moved it there), not something you'd reach for to mark tomorrow off. There is no per-date override anywhere in the schema.

Consequence: every mechanism that reasons about "today" or "this week" — `WorkdaySessionProvider.start()` (falls back to a template and generates a full day regardless), `buildLoadIndexSnapshot`/`computeRequiredMinutes` (`lib/loadindex.ts:43-50`, expects the usual course/revision/goal minutes out of the day), `pickNextAction` (`lib/next-action.ts`, will still propose a task), and — the one this doc is really about — `/plan/week`'s Step 4, has no way to know a given date is off-limits, so it treats it exactly like any other day. A day the owner has decided not to work reads as "Behind" on Workload and is exactly as schedulable as any other day to the weekly planner — which, per §2.2, actively makes it a *magnet* for proposals rather than a day they're excluded from.

### 2.2 Step 4 piles proposals onto whichever day looks emptiest, worked example

`placeProposals` (`app/(app)/plan/week/page.tsx:100-127`) is the entire placement algorithm. For each candidate, in the order candidates were generated (§2.3), it does this:

```ts
const searchDates = candidate.dateKey
  ? [candidate.dateKey]
  : [...workingDates].sort((a, b) => totalClaimedMinutes(simulated[a]) - totalClaimedMinutes(simulated[b]));
```

For any candidate without a fixed `dateKey` — which, per §2.3, is *every* course-bucket chunk, every backlog task, and every stale-paper reading — the day search order is "sort every working day by how many minutes are already claimed on it, ascending." The first day in that order with a free gap wins (`lib/weekplan.ts`'s `placeInWindow`), the placed block's minutes are added to `simulated[day]` (line 122-123), and the loop moves to the next candidate. There is no cap, no round-robin, no memory that a day has already absorbed several candidates in this same pass — the only thing that ever stops a day from winning again is its own running total catching up to its neighbors'.

Worked example, a real-shaped week: two courses, "CS201" (revision 3h/week, assignment 2h/week) and "MATH210" (revision 2h/week), `workMinutes` = 25 (the default). Monday through Friday each already carry ~2h of routine blocks (meals) inside the 09:00–18:00 working window — a normal week — except **Thursday, which has nothing on it** (the owner hasn't touched it yet, or it's a day they in fact don't intend to work, with no way to say so per §2.1). `claimedSlotsForDate` (`app/(app)/plan/week/page.tsx:197-204`) reports Thursday's committed total as ~0 minutes against every other day's ~120.

- Candidate 1 (CS201 revision, 25min): sorted list puts Thursday first (0 < 120). Placed 09:00–09:25 Thursday. Thursday's running total: 25.
- Candidates 2 through 7 (the rest of CS201's 3h revision, 4×25min plus rounding, per `chunks = Math.round(remaining / workMinutes)`, `app/(app)/plan/week/page.tsx:294`): Thursday is still under 120 minutes until its 5th block lands (125 > 120). All land on Thursday.
- Candidates 8–12 (CS201's 2h assignment, ~5 chunks): by now Thursday is at 125–250 minutes, finally comparable to the other days' 120 — but still frequently *equal or lower* than a day whose own blocks haven't grown yet, so several of these land on Thursday too, some spill onto whichever other day is next-lowest.
- Candidates for MATH210's 2h revision (5 chunks): by now Thursday is solidly the most-loaded day in the *simulated* state and finally stops winning — these spread across the remaining days.

Net: a day that was empty because nobody had planned it yet (or, per the owner's own hypothesis, because it's actually a holiday) absorbs roughly the first 10–12 of ~17 candidates — the bulk of the week's course work — before the algorithm self-corrects. This is not a rare edge case; it is the *typical* case for the first week anyone uses `/plan/week`, because every day starts equally empty except for whatever routine blocks exist, and ties resolve toward array order (`Array.prototype.sort`'s stability), so the least-populated day at the start of the pass keeps winning until it visibly isn't the least-populated one anymore.

`scripts/verify-weekplan.ts` covers `weekCapacity` and `placeInWindow` (the single-candidate, single-day gap search) in isolation, correctly. It has no test at all for `placeProposals`'s multi-candidate distribution — the exact function this bug lives in was never exercised with more than one candidate in the whole verify suite. That's the gap this doc closes in §5.2, with tests.

### 2.3 Nothing about candidate order or type is holiday- or lecture-aware

From `app/(app)/plan/week/page.tsx:276-312`, the five candidate sources, in generation order:

1. Checkpoint prep — **has** a fixed `dateKey` per day of the prep window (line 283).
2. Course buckets (revision/assignment/backlog) — **floating**, no `dateKey`, no relationship to `course.sessions` (the course's own weekly lecture times, already on every `Course` document and already read elsewhere by `courseSlotsForDate`, `lib/courses.ts:15`) even for the revision bucket, whose entire purpose is reviewing that course's material.
3. The revision queue (spaced-repetition due items) — **has** a fixed `dateKey`, one block per weekday (line 300).
4. Backlog tasks (open, no due date or past due) — **floating**, no day-of-week preference at all, so a leftover task is exactly as likely to land on a Tuesday as a Saturday.
5. Stale papers (reading, untouched 14+ days) — **floating**, fixed 45-minute duration, no user input on *when* it should be read, unlike a checkpoint (which has `dueAt`/`prepLeadDays`) or a due-dated task.

So the two most requested behaviors — "revise near the lecture" and "leftover work on the weekend" — aren't unimplementable gaps in a good algorithm; they're candidates that were never given the day-of-week hint the algorithm would need to honor them, because nothing in `WeekProposalCandidate` (`app/(app)/plan/week/page.tsx:69-80`) carries a *preferred* day short of a *fixed* one. The type only has `dateKey?: string` — hard-pinned or nothing.

## 3. Scenarios

### S1 — Marking Friday off, in advance

Sunday evening, opening `/plan/week` for the coming week. Step 2 ("The week already contains") now has a small control on each day card — *Mark as day off*. Friday: family event, marking it off. The day card grays out and reads "Day off" instead of its routine blocks. The capacity meter at the top, which a second ago read *Committed 14h of 45h free*, updates to *Committed 14h of 39h free* — Friday's ~6h of working-window time is gone from the denominator, not just visually struck through. Proceeding to Step 4: nothing gets proposed for Friday, at all — not even as a low-priority filler. → §4, §5.1, §5.2

### S2 — Reporting a holiday after the fact

Wednesday. National holiday — Tuesday was actually a holiday and nothing got done, but the schedule still show Tuesday's blocks as merely "past," not accounted for anywhere. Opening `/plan/day`, going back to Tuesday, marking it a day off (same control, works on a past date same as a future one). The Workload snapshot for Tuesday — already computed and stored (`14` §6.5, `days/{date}.loadIndex`) — doesn't retroactively change (it's a historical record), but Look back's stats on the next `/plan/week` visit now show *1 day off this week*, and Tuesday's `elapsedSlots`/`uncoveredBlocks` count stops nagging about blocks that "timed out without being marked," because a day-off date is excluded from that check too. → §5.1, §5.5

### S3 — Revision lands on the lecture day

CS201 meets Tuesday and Thursday. Revision target: 2h/week. Opening Step 4: two 50-minute "CS201 — Revision" blocks, one Tuesday, one Thursday, placed in the free gap nearest the lecture's own end time — not scattered across Monday/Wednesday/Friday because those happened to be emptier. If Thursday's lecture slot has no room after it (a packed day), the second block falls back to the next best lecture day this week, and only falls back further, to the general least-loaded search, if there isn't one. → §5.2

### S4 — Leftover work lands on the weekend

Three backlog tasks with no due date, sitting in Tasks since last month. Step 4 proposes all three for Saturday and Sunday, in that order, only spilling onto a weekday if the weekend genuinely has no room left after everything else the week already contains. → §5.2

### S5 — A reading gets a target day

A paper's been sitting in "reading" for three weeks. Step 3 (or a small new "Reading targets" row under it) asks: *target day for this read?* — a day-of-week picker, defaulting to a sensible mid-week day. Wednesday, picking. Step 4 proposes two 30-minute reading blocks, one Monday, one Wednesday — split across the lead time the same way checkpoint prep splits `prepEstimateMin` across `prepLeadDays` — landing *by* Wednesday, not dumped as one 45-minute block on whatever day was free. → §5.3

## 4. Data model changes

Two additive fields — one on `Day`, one extending `WeekProposalCandidate` (a local type in `app/(app)/plan/week/page.tsx`, not persisted, so it's not really a "data model" change in the migration sense, listed here for completeness).

```ts
// types/index.ts — Day, additive
export interface Day {
  // ...existing fields unchanged...
  /** plan/15 §4 — this date is a day off, marked in advance or reported after the fact. Absent
   *  (the default, same convention every other optional Day field uses) means an ordinary day.
   *  Read by the weekly planner (excluded from placement and from the capacity meter's `free`
   *  term for that date), Workload (`computeRequiredMinutes` returns 0), Up next, and Start day. */
  dayOff?: {
    reason?: string;
    /** Distinguishes "I decided in advance" from "reporting after the fact" only for display
     *  (Look back's stat, §5.1) — both work identically everywhere else. */
    markedAt: string;
  };
}
```

```ts
// app/(app)/plan/week/page.tsx — WeekProposalCandidate, additive (not persisted — see §5.2/§5.3)
interface WeekProposalCandidate {
  // ...existing fields unchanged...
  /** plan/15 §5.2/§5.3 — a *preferred* day (unlike `dateKey`, which is a hard pin): try this date
   *  first, fall back to the general search if it has no room, instead of either "must be exactly
   *  here" or "no opinion at all." */
  preferredDateKey?: string;
  /** plan/15 §5.2 — try these days, in order, before falling back to the general least-loaded
   *  search. Used for "the weekend, in order" and "this course's lecture days, in order" — a list
   *  rather than a single `preferredDateKey` because both of those are themselves ordered
   *  preferences, not one date. */
  preferredDateKeys?: string[];
}
```

**Why `dayOff` lives on `Day`, not as a new field on `DailySchedule` or a locked full-day `ScheduleSlot`.** `Day` is already the single document every date-scoped read in the app subscribes to (`session`, `review`, `loadIndex`, `brief`, `rollup` — `14` §7.3 leaned on this same fact for `startDayNotice`). A full-day locked slot was considered and rejected: it would have to interact with `validateSlots`'s overlap check (blocking every other block that date, which is *correct* behavior but awkward to special-case out of the editor's normal "you can't overlap a locked block" refusal), and it would live on `DailySchedule`, a document several of the readers above (Workload, Up next) don't otherwise touch. A boolean-ish field on `Day` is one flag, read wherever it's needed, no interaction with the schedule editor's own invariants.

**Why `dayOff` is optional and not, say, a three-state enum with "working."** Same "absent means the default" convention every other optional field in this schema uses (`14` §4 made the identical case for `weeklyPlanning`/`workingWindow`). Nothing needs to distinguish "explicitly marked as a working day" from "never marked" — there's no reader that would do anything differently for the two.

## 5. Design

### 5.1 Marking a day off, and where the stat shows up

**Setting it.** One new `lib/firestore.ts` function, `setDayOff(uid, date, reason?)`, going through the now-fixed `saveDayFields` (`14` §12 — the dot-path nesting bug that would have made this feature land the same way `session.startedAt` did before that fix) — `saveDayFields(uid, date, { dayOff: { reason, markedAt: now() } })` — and `clearDayOff(uid, date)` calling `saveDayFields(uid, date, { dayOff: FieldValue.delete() })` (the existing `deleteField()` pattern `undoEndWorkdaySession` already uses, `lib/firestore.ts:796-798`).

**Where the control lives.** Three places, all cheap because they already render a per-day surface:
- `/plan/week` Step 2's day cards (S1) — the natural spot, since that's where the coming week is laid out.
- `/plan/day` (any date) — a button next to "Running late?" (S2, the after-the-fact report path).
- The dashboard's Now card (`14` §7.2) — when today itself is marked off, replaces the whole card with a short "Day off" state instead of a current block (mirrors the "No plan for today" empty state already there).

**What reading it changes.**
- `/plan/week`'s `claimedSlotsByDate` — a day-off date's contribution to `weekCapacity`'s `freeMinutes` becomes 0 outright (excluded from `workingWindow.daysOfWeek` for that week's computation, not merely "fully claimed" — the distinction matters for the meter's wording, "free" vs "committed" would otherwise both undercount from the same cause).
- `placeProposals` (§5.2) — day-off dates are removed from `workingDates` entirely, so no candidate, floating or preferred, can land there.
- `computeRequiredMinutes` (`lib/loadindex.ts:43`) — gains an `isDayOff` input; returns 0 when true, short-circuiting before summing the other four terms, so Workload reads a clean day rather than "Behind."
- `pickNextAction` (`lib/next-action.ts`) — gains the same input; returns a `kind: "day-off"` action (`why: "Marked as a day off"`) ahead of every other branch, mirroring §7.1's `kind: "block"` short-circuit in `14`.
- `WorkdaySessionProvider.start()` — if today is marked off, skips the template-fallback generation entirely and sets a `startDayNotice` explaining why, rather than building a full day nobody intends to work.
- `components/uncovered-blocks-review.tsx` (`14`'s "Blocks that timed out" card) — a day-off date's blocks are never uncovered-by-definition; the query simply doesn't render for that date.

**The stat.** Look back (`/plan/week` Step 1, `14` §5.1) gains one tile, *Days off*, counting `Day` documents in the outgoing week with `dayOff` set — the same client-side count-from-already-fetched-`days` pattern `14` already uses for catch-up hours and streak (`priorWeekDays.filter(...)`, `app/(app)/plan/week/page.tsx`). A second, longer-window count (e.g. this month, or since term start) is a reasonable follow-up but not required for this pass — §8 flags whether it's worth a dedicated place (Analytics?) rather than just Look back's weekly number.

### 5.2 Redesigned placement: exclusions, per-day caps, and preferred days

`placeProposals` changes in four ways, each independent and each testable in isolation:

**1. Day-off dates are removed from `workingDates` before anything else runs.** One filter, at the top of the function, against the same date list §5.1 already has fetching `Day` docs for. This alone fixes the "probably holiday" half of the bug directly — an off day is no longer *reachable*, so it can't win the least-loaded contest regardless of how empty it looks.

**2. A per-day cap on floating candidates, so an empty-but-working day can't absorb the whole week either.** The root cause traced in §2.2 isn't specific to holidays — any day that's merely *unusually* empty (the owner just hasn't planned it yet) has the same magnet effect. Cap: no more than `ceil(totalFloatingCandidateMinutes / eligibleWorkingDays.length) × 1.5` minutes of *newly proposed* floating work per day — a soft ceiling 50% above the even share, generous enough that a genuinely lighter day still takes more than its exact share (matching how a person would actually plan — "Tuesday's emptier, I'll put more there, but not *everything*"), tight enough that one day can no longer take 10 of 17 candidates. A day at its cap is removed from the sort for the rest of the pass (not just deprioritized — otherwise a large `durationMinutes` candidate near the end could still push it over by a lot). Days pinned via `dateKey` (checkpoint prep, the revision queue) are exempt from the cap — they were never part of the problem, and capping them would just move the §2.2 bug into "checkpoint prep now doesn't fit" territory.

**3. Preferred days, tried before the general search, honored only if they have room.** Using the new `preferredDateKeys` (§4):
   - **Revision candidates** get `preferredDateKeys` = that course's lecture days this week (`courseSlotsForDate([course], d).length > 0` for each `d` in `weekDateKeys`, gated by `isBreakMode` the same way `14` §6.1/§6.3 already gate every other `courseSlotsForDate` call), sorted by proximity to the lecture's own end time so the review block lands *after* class rather than merely *on* the right day. A course with no sessions this week (mid-break, or an async course with no `CourseSession` at all) has no preferred days and falls through to the general search unchanged — this is strictly additive, never a regression from today's behavior.
   - **Backlog-bucket and backlog-task candidates** get `preferredDateKeys` = `[Sat, Sun]` for this week (the last two entries of `weekDateKeys`, respecting the Mon-start convention `14` already established), in that order.
   - Placement tries each `preferredDateKeys` entry in order (still subject to the day-off exclusion and the per-day cap from #1/#2), then falls through to the existing least-loaded sort among whatever's left — never a hard failure just because the preferred days are full. A block that only fit off its preferred day still shows in Step 4 exactly as before; nothing about "Didn't fit" (`14` §5.4's honesty rule) changes.

**4. Candidate generation order changes slightly**, so revision and backlog run *last*, after assignment. This matters given #2's cap: assignment work has no day preference of its own, so it should fill in around the days revision and backlog have already claimed via their preferences, not the reverse (today's order — revision first — meant assignment's floating chunks had first pick of whatever was left, and revision's chunks, despite being the ones this doc wants pinned to lecture days, went in before there was even a distinction to honor).

`scripts/verify-weekplan.ts` gains coverage this bug's absence let slip through: a multi-candidate `placeProposals` case with one empty day among several populated ones, asserting no single day receives more than the capped share; a revision candidate with a known lecture day, asserting it lands there over an emptier non-lecture day; a backlog candidate, asserting it prefers Saturday over a lighter Tuesday; and a day-off date, asserting zero candidates land there regardless of how empty it looks.

### 5.3 Reading and task target days

A small new control, in Step 3 alongside the hours table (`14` §5.3) or as its own short row directly above Step 4 — one per stale paper (`14` §5.4 point 5) and, where relevant, one per undated backlog task: a day-of-week picker, *Target day*, defaulting to a mid-week day (Wednesday, matching the owner's own example) the first time, remembered per-paper/per-task afterward (a new optional field, `Paper.weeklyTargetDay?: number` / `Task.weeklyTargetDay?: number` — 0–6, same convention as `RoutineBlock.daysOfWeek` — written from this control, read here and nowhere else yet).

Placement, once a target day is set: split the item's total duration across the days from "now" through the target day inside this week — the exact `buildProposedSlots`/checkpoint-prep pattern (`14` §5.4 point 1, itself lifted from `lib/dailyloop.ts:163`), just applied to a paper/task instead of a checkpoint. A reading with no target day set keeps today's behavior (one floating 45-minute block, general search) — this is opt-in per item, not a required extra step before Step 4 will show anything.

### 5.4 Heuristics vs. an LLM call — recommendation

The owner's own question. Recommendation: **heuristics, as designed in §5.2/§5.3, are the fix — not a stopgap for it.** Three reasons, all specific to this bug rather than a general aversion to AI:

1. **The bug is a heuristic bug.** "Which day should this land on" has a correct, fully deterministic answer once the day-off/lecture-day/weekend/target-day inputs exist — there's no ambiguity an LLM's judgment would resolve better than an explicit rule. An LLM call would be solving a problem that's already solved by giving the existing algorithm the inputs it's missing.
2. **It's the standing rule this exact plan-folder pass already committed to.** `14` §1: *"No new AI. Every mechanism here is deterministic, consistent with the codebase's own 'no feature is AI-only' rule."* Overturning that for the one piece of `14` that turned out to have a bug, rather than fixing the bug, would be a strange place to make the exception.
3. **Determinism is the actual feature here.** Part of what made `14`'s capacity meter trustworthy (§5.3's own reasoning: "a meter that opens at 28h of 89h free tells the user the opposite of what this step exists to produce") is that placement and the meter share one set of bounds and can never disagree. An LLM call introduces a second source of "where things go" that the deterministic meter has no way to stay in sync with, unless the LLM's own output is fed back through the same `validateSlots`/capacity math anyway — at which point it's an expensive way to produce candidates the heuristic could propose directly.

That said, the codebase already has real precedent for an *optional*, additive AI layer next to a working deterministic one — `/plan/day`'s "Create with AI" box (`components/plan/plan-tray.tsx:424-430`, a natural-language day-template generator that sits alongside, not instead of, the built-in catalog and the manual editor). A parallel **Phase 3, genuinely optional**, described but not designed in depth here: a "Suggest a different arrangement" button in Step 4, after the deterministic pass has already populated proposals, that sends the same candidate list plus constraints to an AI task and offers its rearrangement as an alternative set of proposals — still subject to the same `validateSlots` gate before anything can be accepted, still fully replaceable by re-running the deterministic pass. Never the only path to a populated Step 4; never blocking Commit if the AI call fails or the account has no AI budget (same "degrades gracefully" rule the rest of the app's AI features already follow — see `MorningBriefCard`'s `degraded` handling for precedent). This is explicitly **not** part of Phase 1/2 below; it's named so it isn't relitigated as "did we consider this" later.

### 5.5 Interaction with `14`'s own uncovered-block/Workload machinery

Two small consistency fixes, both direct consequences of §5.1 rather than new design:

- `uncovered-blocks-review.tsx`'s query for "blocks that timed out" already only shows for `date === todayKey()` (`14` §6.5's own scoping decision). Add: also skip when that date's `Day.dayOff` is set — a day nobody intended to work has no blocks that meaningfully "timed out."
- `computeDebtHours`/`computeLoadIndexStreak` (`lib/loadindex.ts`) read historical `loadIndex` snapshots, which are written once, at End Day (`14` §6.5), and don't change retroactively when a day is marked off after the fact (S2). This is deliberate — a day-off marking changes how a day is *planned and read going forward*, not a rewrite of what already happened — but it does mean a day marked off *after* an End Day snapshot exists will still show as "Behind" in the historical catch-up-hours number. Documented here rather than silently inconsistent; §8 asks whether that's the right call.

## 6. Phasing

1. **Phase 1 — days off.** §4's `Day.dayOff` field, `setDayOff`/`clearDayOff`, the three UI entry points (§5.1), and every reader listed there (capacity meter, `placeProposals` exclusion, `computeRequiredMinutes`, `pickNextAction`, `WorkdaySessionProvider.start()`, the uncovered-blocks query). Ships independently — it's useful on its own even before Phase 2's placement redesign, and it's the one piece of this doc that's a pure gap-fill rather than a fix to existing behavior.
2. **Phase 2 — placement redesign.** §5.2 (exclusions already done by Phase 1; the cap, preferred-day search, and generation-order change are new) and §5.3 (reading/task target days). This is the actual fix for the owner's complaint; Phase 1 alone only fixes the "probably holiday" half of it — an ordinary empty day would still be a magnet without Phase 2's per-day cap.
3. **Phase 3 — optional AI-assisted rearrangement** (§5.4). Not scoped in detail here; a follow-up doc if it's wanted after Phase 1/2 are in real use and someone still wants a second opinion on top of them.

Phase 2 must not ship without Phase 1 — the cap and preferred-day logic both assume day-off dates are already out of the candidate pool; retrofitting the exclusion after tuning the cap's percentage would mean re-tuning it.

## 7. Acceptance criteria

1. Marking a date off (future or past) removes it from `/plan/week`'s `workingDates` for that week; the capacity meter's `free` drops by exactly that date's working-window minutes, not by "however much was already claimed on it."
2. With a date marked off, Step 4 proposes zero blocks for it, regardless of how many candidates are floating and how empty every other day is.
3. §2.2's worked example, re-run against the Phase 2 algorithm: no single day receives more than the capped share of floating candidates; the two-course week's revision blocks land on lecture days when the course has sessions that week.
4. A backlog task with no due date is proposed for Saturday or Sunday before any weekday, unless the weekend has no room left after everything else that week already contains.
5. A paper with a target day set gets its reading time split across the days leading up to that target, landing by it — not one floating 45-minute block placed anywhere in the week.
6. Workload's required-minutes for a day marked off is 0; `pickNextAction` returns the day-off action ahead of every other branch; Start day on a marked-off date does not generate a template-fallback schedule.
7. Every existing document still loads with `Day.dayOff` absent, `Paper.weeklyTargetDay`/`Task.weeklyTargetDay` absent — the current behavior for all three, unchanged, is the default.

New pure-function verify coverage, matching `14`'s own convention: `scripts/verify-weekplan.ts` gains the four `placeProposals` cases named in §5.2's last paragraph (multi-candidate cap, lecture-day preference, weekend preference, day-off exclusion) — the exact gap identified in §2.2 that let this bug ship unnoticed.

## 8. Open questions

- **Is a soft per-day cap (§5.2 #2) enough, or does this eventually want a real search** (try the week, backtrack when a preferred day is full, minimize total "distance from preference" across all candidates rather than resolving greedily candidate-by-candidate)? The cap fixes the specific bug reported; a person planning by hand does something closer to a search. Worth a second pass once Phase 2 has a week or two of real use, not before.
- **Should "days off" carry a standing weekly budget** (e.g., "I take 1 day off most weeks, flag it if I'm about to take a 3rd this month") rather than just being counted after the fact? §1 deliberately scoped this out as a non-goal; flagging it because "store such stats" could reasonably be read either way.
- **A day-off marking made *after* an End Day snapshot already exists** (S2) doesn't rewrite that day's historical Workload number (§5.5). Is that the right call, or should marking a day off retroactively also zero out its stored `loadIndex` so `computeDebtHours`/streak stop counting it as a missed day?
- **§5.2's revision-to-lecture-day matching uses `course.sessions`, not attendance.** A course with sessions configured but `isBreakMode` currently true (no covering term — the exact `L4` situation `14` §2.4 fixed a warning for) has no *materialized* class blocks this week even though `course.sessions` exists — should revision still prefer the nominal session days in that case, or fall through to the general search the same as a course with no sessions at all? Leaning toward "fall through" (matching the spirit of `L4`'s fix — a session that won't show on the timeline shouldn't drive placement either), but worth deciding explicitly before Phase 2 builds the matching logic.
- **Where does the longer-window "days off" count belong** (§5.1's stat) — just Look back's weekly tile, or does it deserve a home in Analytics (`14` never touched that page) alongside the existing 30-day trend charts?
