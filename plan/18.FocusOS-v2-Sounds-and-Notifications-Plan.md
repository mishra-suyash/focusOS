# FocusOS v2 — Sounds, and notifications that arrive once

**Status:** Implemented (Phases 0–5) on 2026-10-27. §9 records what was built differently from this spec, and what was and wasn't verified in the running app. **Where §9 disagrees with an earlier section, §9 wins.**
**Routes touched:** every route, through root-level providers. In particular `/settings` (the Reminders card) and `/read` (it gains reminders it has never had). Files: `components/focus-session-provider.tsx`, `components/workday-session-provider.tsx`, `app/layout.tsx`, `app/(app)/layout.tsx`, `app/(app)/settings/page.tsx`, `types/index.ts` (`UserSettings`). New: `lib/sounds.ts`, `lib/notify.ts`, `components/toast-host.tsx`.
**Companion to:**
- `02` §12, whose anti-feature rule this doc narrows in the open (§1).
- `01` §"Cut list", which said of the old sound toggle: *"Either implement with a 3-line Web Audio oscillator or delete the icon. Do not ship a fake control."*
- `08` (the floating widget, which must never become a reminders feed).
- `16` §5.7 (sitting notifications, the last doc to narrow §12).

**Evidence basis:** I read the source at `b6a2666` (HEAD). Every current-state claim is marked **[code]** with `file:line`. **None of these claims has been walked in the running app yet.** Phase 0's first step does that: it reproduces F1–F4 in Chrome on the test account before anything is changed, and §7 lists the criteria that can only be settled there.

## 0. TL;DR

**The brief:** do a detailed review of where sounds can be implemented, and of in-app notifications too, and make a plan.

**What the review found:**

1. **No sound anywhere.** There is no `Audio`, no `AudioContext` and no asset folder (there is no `public/` at all) **[code]**.
2. **The moment a focus session ends is silent.**
   - The timer reaching zero raises no sound and no OS notification **[code]** `focus-session-provider.tsx:563-570, 627-669`.
   - If the floating widget is closed and you're in another app, nothing tells you the sitting is over.
   - The same is true when a break ends.
   - This is the single biggest gap, and it is what a focus timer exists for.
3. **The notifications that do exist have defects.** Adding a sound to them unchanged would make each defect audible:
   - **F1.** An unanswered hydration or break reminder fires a fresh OS notification every 30 seconds until you click it (§2.2).
   - **F2.** Each open tab fires its own copy of every reminder and block-start notification (§2.2).
   - **F3.** The block-start notification ignores every setting and announces sleep and meal blocks (§2.2).
   - **F4.** `/read` mounts no `WorkdaySessionProvider`, so while you read in the reader you get no reminders and no sitting notifications (§2.3).
4. **Some in-app events are silent or misleading** (§2.4):
   - A focus session that fails to save only logs to the console, and the survey still appears as if it saved.
   - Starting a new sitting while one runs logs the old one, and nothing says so.

**The plan, in four ideas:**

1. **Sound is a cue, not a feed.** A small set of public-domain recordings (Kenney CC0 cues and Wikimedia Commons noise; see §9), at the handful of moments listed in §3. The timer-end cue is on by default and everything else is opt-in.
2. **One tab speaks.** Every sound and every notification is gated by the same cross-tab claim the session log already uses (`claimRun`), extended to breaks and reminders. Two tabs means one chime.
3. **Fix the existing notifications first.** Phase 0 fixes F1–F4 before any sound lands, because a sound on F1 would be a beep every 30 seconds.
4. **In-app notifications mean a transient toast, not an inbox.** One toast host is used for three things: save failures, an auto-logged sitting, and a block that just closed. There is no history and no bell icon (§1).

**Estimate:** about **26 focused hours, roughly 4 working days**, for Phases 0–4. Optional Phase 5 (ambient focus sound) adds about 1 day. More than half of each phase is browser verification (§8).

## 1. The rule this doc narrows, and the rule it keeps

`02` §12 says, as an anti-feature:

> Notifications outside the hydration/break loop — every extra notification devalues the ones that matter, and this is a study tool, not a chat app.

`08` treats that rule as binding (`08:68`), and `16` §5.7 narrowed it once, for sitting start and end (`16:541-544`). This doc narrows it a second time, and only for **one new OS notification: the end of a countdown you started yourself.** That passes `02`'s own test:

- You asked for it by pressing Start.
- It fires once per countdown.
- It is the event the timer exists to report.

Everything else here either:

- is not a notification (a sound, or a toast inside a tab you are looking at); or
- makes existing notifications fire *less* often (Phase 0).

**Kept, without change:**

- **No notification centre, bell, unread count or history.** That would be a second inbox, which `02` §12 bans separately ("One capture point. The moment there are two, both rot").
- **The PiP never makes sound and never notifies.** As `16` §5.7 established for notifications, everything fires from the main document. The PiP is a separate `Window`, it is Chromium-only, and it may be closed.
- **No praise sounds, no streak sounds, no sound for a missed sitting.** A block ending unfinished is a fact about a block, not a verdict on the person (`16` §5.6). The end-of-sitting sound is the same sound whatever the outcome.
- **No *new* OS notification is on by default, and no sound other than the timer-end and break-end chimes is.** The existing block-start notification keeps today's default (N3).

### 2.2 Defects in what exists

- **F1. A reminder repeats every 30 seconds until you answer it.** **[code]**
  - The reminder interval runs every 30 s (`:117, :147`). Once `now - lastHydrationRef >= hydrationMinutes` it calls `fireReminder` and returns (`:135-137`).
  - `lastHydrationRef` moves only on acknowledge or dismiss (`:411-429`) or when the Firestore session changes (`:95-100`).
  - `fireReminder` guards the *banner* with `current ?? …` but calls `new Notification(…)` unconditionally (`:104-109`), with no `tag`.
  - Result: leave a hydration reminder unanswered for five minutes and you get ten stacked OS notifications. The break reminder behaves the same way.
- **F2. Each tab fires its own copy.** **[code]**
  - `WorkdaySessionProvider` mounts once per tab (`app/(app)/layout.tsx:7`), and each copy runs its own interval and its own refs.
  - Two FocusOS tabs mean two hydration notifications, and two "X starting." for each block (no `tag`).
  - Sitting lead and end notifications carry a `tag` (`:197, :213`), so Chrome replaces the duplicate on screen. A sound wired to the same code path would still play twice.
  - **"Skip" doesn't propagate.** "Did it" writes `session.lastHydrationAt` (`lib/firestore.ts:836`), so every tab re-seeds. "Skip" only moves this tab's ref (`:424-429`), so the other tab keeps reminding.
  - The owner usually has a FocusOS tab of their own open (memory, 2026-10-14), so in practice this is the normal case, not an edge case.
- **F3. The block-start notification is ungated.** **[code]** `:125-133`
  - It fires for every block type, including `sleep`, `meal` and `free`, whenever permission is granted.
  - Permission is requested on *Start day* (`:235-236`). So anyone who said yes once, and still has the day started with a tab open, gets "Sleep starting." and "Lunch starting.".
  - It is not covered by the Settings toggle, and the Settings copy doesn't mention it.
- **F4. `/read` is a dead zone.** **[code]**
  - `app/read/[paperId]/page.tsx` sits outside `app/(app)/`, deliberately (`:14-20`), so it never mounts `WorkdaySessionProvider`.
  - `FocusSessionProvider` was hoisted to the root for exactly this reason (`app/layout.tsx:19-21`); the workday provider was not.
  - Reading in the reader as your only tab therefore means no hydration or break reminders and no sitting lead or end notifications.
  - Settings already hedges this: "Reminders only fire while your day is started and this tab is open" (`settings/page.tsx:119`).
- **F5. Clicking a notification does nothing.** No `onclick` handler focuses the tab or navigates anywhere.

### 2.3 Constraints any sound has to work within

- **C1. Autoplay policy.**
  - An `AudioContext` starts `suspended` until the document has had a user gesture.
  - The Start click is that gesture for the timer chime.
  - A hydration reminder 45 minutes after a cold page load, with no click since, may play silently.
  - **Rule:** create and resume the context on the first pointer or keydown in the main document. A context that is still not `running` means no in-app sound from that tab.
  - **Which tab plays is decided separately from which tab logs (§5.2).** A tab that adopted the session through `storage` and was never clicked must not win the right to play, or the chime that is on by default goes silent.
  - If no tab has a running context, the only cue is the OS notification, **and only if the user turned N1 on**. That gap is stated in Settings copy, not hidden. On macOS, an OS notification plays the system sound unless it is `silent`.
  - **Unverified:** whether a click inside the PiP counts as a gesture for the opener's AudioContext. Starting from the PiP is the main way this feature gets used, so AC#8 tests it first.
- **C2. Hidden-tab throttling.**
  - Completion is detected on a 1 s interval (`focus-session-provider.tsx:546-561`).
  - After about 5 minutes hidden, Chrome throttles that interval to about once a minute, so with the PiP closed the chime could arrive up to about 60 s late. With the PiP open it is on time, because the PiP drives its own tick (`floating-widget-content.tsx:46-52`, verified 2026-10-14).
  - The only clock Chrome does not throttle is the audio clock. A cue scheduled with `osc.start(ctx.currentTime + secondsLeft)` lands exactly on time.
  - **Decision:** Phase 2 measures the real lag first. If it is over 10 s, the chime is pre-scheduled on the audio clock at arm time (§5.2). Otherwise the chime fires at detection.
- **C3. The PiP is a separate document.** It has its own (unlocked) audio policy and can close at any moment. All sound plays from the main document (§1).
- **C4. Cross-tab.** `claimRun(runId)` (`focus-session-provider.tsx:228-240`) already elects exactly one tab per completed work session, through a Web Lock. Breaks never call it (`:629-631`), and reminders have nothing equivalent.

### 2.4 In-app events that are silent or misleading

- **I1. A failed save looks like a success.** `persistWorkSession` shows the survey *before* the save, and on failure only calls `console.error` (`:679-687`). A failed rating save does the same (`:784-785`).
- **I2. An auto-logged sitting gives no feedback.** Starting a sitting while another runs logs the old one with `survey: false` (`:708-710`). That is correct, but you never learn that 18 minutes were credited to the previous block.
- **I3. A block closing gives no feedback.** `syncSlotAutoStatus` flips a block to `completed` once 60% of it is covered (`:250-269`). That is the day score's unit of progress (`lib/gamify.ts`), and it happens with no signal at all.

Error reporting elsewhere is already good: `recurring-commitment-row.tsx:28-48` and others show inline errors, and `use-day-autosave.ts` has a status pill. I1 is the main gap. This doc does **not** replace those inline patterns with toasts.

## 3. Where sound goes

*(Superseded, see §9: the sounds are public-domain audio files in `public/sounds/`, played through `<audio>`, not synthesized.)* One master volume applies, plus a per-cue toggle.

| # | Moment | Cue | Default | Fires from | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Focus countdown **reaches 0 on its own** | `sessionEnd`: two rising notes | **On** | The winner of `claimCue("play:" + runId)` among tabs whose audio is `running` (§5.2), which is not necessarily the `claimRun` winner | The same sound whether or not the block closed (§1). |
| S2 | Break countdown **reaches 0 on its own** | `breakEnd`: one soft note | **On** | As S1 | — |

**One rule for ends the user triggers:** no cue. That covers *Finish now* (work or break), *Skip break*, *Reset*, and `startFocus`'s auto-log (I2), because each is your own click and you're already looking at the app. The zero-crossing effect (`focus-session-provider.tsx:563-570`) passes `{ natural: true }` into `complete()`, and only that path cues. `complete()` itself stays cue-free, because *Finish now* also runs through it (`:617-620`).
| S3 | Sitting lead (N min before) | `heads-up`: soft tick | Off | The winner of `claimCue(leadKey)` | Only when `sittingNotifications` is on. Same trigger as the existing notification. |
| S4 | Hydration / break reminder raised | `reminder`: a water-drop sound | Off | The winner of `claimCue(reminderKey)` | Once per reminder, never per tick (F1). |
| S5 | Block closes (I3) | `blockClosed`: a short, quiet confirmation | Off | The tab that logged the session | The only "progress" sound, and deliberately the quietest. |
| — | Timer start, pause, button clicks, survey save | none | — | — | UI click sounds are noise. Considered and rejected. |
| — | Sitting ended unfinished, a lost streak | none | — | — | §1: no judgement sounds. |
| P5 | Ambient focus sound (brown or pink noise) while a focus countdown runs | Loop | Off | The main document | **Optional, Phase 5.** Not a notification, so it doesn't touch §12. It also keeps a hidden tab out of Chrome's intensive throttling, as a side effect we don't rely on. |

**Global gates for every sound:** `settings.sounds.enabled`; `day?.dayOff` mutes S3–S5 (the timer still chimes, because you started it); and an `AudioContext` that is not `running` means silent, falling back to the OS notification (C1).

## 4. Notifications: what changes

| # | Notification | Change |
|---|---|---|
| N1 | **New:** "Focus done — 25 min on CS201 Revision. Break: 5 min." / "Break over." | Opt-in (`notifications.timerEnd`, default **off**). Fired by the `claimRun` / `claimCue` winner, with `tag: timer:${runId}`. **Skipped when `document.hasFocus()` is true in any FocusOS tab**, because then you're looking at the survey already. That check uses the winner's own `document.hasFocus()` plus a `focusos-focused-at` heartbeat in localStorage. |
| N2 | Hydration / break reminder | Fixes F1: fire once per reminder, with `tag: reminder:${kind}:${dateKey}`. Fixes F2: elect one tab with `claimCue`, and make "Skip" write a timestamp to Firestore (`session.lastHydrationAt` or `session.lastBreakPromptAt`) so every tab re-seeds. **The decision on that write is in §6, D2.** |
| N3 | Block-start "X starting." | Fixes F3: put it behind `notifications.blockStart`, default **on** (today's behaviour), skip `NO_PROMPT_SLOT_TYPES` (`:37`), and add `tag: start:${dateKey}:${slotId}`. |
| N4 | Sitting lead and end | Unchanged rules. Add `claimCue` so a sound attached to them plays once. |
| N5 | All of the above | Fixes F5: `onclick`, which calls `window.focus()`, closes the notification and, for N1, routes to `/dashboard` if the tab is on an unrelated page. |

One helper does all of this. `lib/notify.ts` exports `notify({ key, title, body, sound?, tag, onClick })`, which:

1. checks the live `Notification.permission`;
2. claims `key` across tabs (a Web Lock and a localStorage "last cued" set with a 12-hour TTL, the same shape as `claimRun`);
3. plays the sound if one is given; then
4. shows the notification.

Every call site in §3 and §4 goes through it, so the dedupe and permission rules exist in one place, not five.

## 5. Design

### 5.1 Data

The data change is additive: both fields are optional, and absent means today's behaviour.

```ts
// types/index.ts — UserSettings
sounds?: {
  enabled: boolean;              // master switch. Absent = { enabled: true, sessionEnd/breakEnd on, rest off }
  volume: number;                // 0–1, default 0.6
  cues: Partial<Record<"sessionEnd" | "breakEnd" | "headsUp" | "reminder" | "blockClosed" | "ambient", boolean>>;
};
notifications?: {
  timerEnd?: boolean;            // N1, default false
  blockStart?: boolean;          // N3, default true (today's behaviour)
};
```

`sittingNotifications` stays where it is (`types/index.ts:1062`). Folding it into `notifications` would mean a migration for no user-visible gain.

### 5.2 Sound engine (`lib/sounds.ts`)

- **One lazily created `AudioContext`.** `unlockAudio()` is registered once on the first `pointerdown` or `keydown` in the main document (in `FocusSessionProvider`, which is mounted at the root). It also runs inside `toggleRunning` and `startFocus`, which are themselves gestures.
- **`playCue(name, volume)`.** Each cue is a small table of `[freq, startOffset, duration]` notes played through a gain envelope. It returns immediately if the context is not `running`.
- **Who plays.** Logging stays with `claimRun`, unchanged. Playing is a separate claim, `claimCue("play:" + runId)`, and **only a tab whose context is `running` competes for it**. A tab with a suspended context never calls it, so it can't win and stay silent. A ready tab always beats one that isn't, whichever tab reached zero first.
- **`scheduleCue(name, atEpochMs)` returns a cancel function.** It is used only if Phase 2's measurement says C2 matters.
  - The armed tab schedules S1 on the audio clock when the countdown is armed.
  - The cue is cancelled when the phase leaves `running` (pause, reset, finish now, or adopting another tab's run).
  - **The cross-tab rule under scheduling:** only the tab where Start was pressed schedules. If that tab closes, the `claimRun` winner falls back to playing at detection.
  - This branch is the one real piece of complexity in the doc, so it's built only if the measurement justifies it.

### 5.3 Covering `/read` (F4)

There are two options:

- **(a) Hoist `WorkdaySessionProvider` into `app/layout.tsx`, beside `FocusSessionProvider`.**
  - It already guards on `user` (`:88`). On `/login`, `/privacy-policy` and the admin pages it would subscribe to nothing.
  - `ReminderBanner` would then also need a slot in the reader's own chrome.
  - Cost: one more `useDay` subscription on the admin pages.
- **(b) A slimmer root-level `<WorkdayNotifier/>`** that only runs the timers.
  - This means duplicating the interval logic or extracting it.

**Recommended: (a).** It is the same move `FocusSessionProvider` already made in plan/17, and it keeps one owner for the day's clock. Risk to check: `useDay(today)` on `/read` must not re-render the PDF canvas. The provider's value is memoized (`:438`), so a change in `day` should not reach the reader unless it consumes the context.

### 5.4 Toasts (`components/toast-host.tsx`)

- **API.** `toast({ message, tone: "info" | "error", action?: { label, onClick }, ms?: number })`, through a context mounted in `app/layout.tsx`. It is per-tab, never synced, holds at most 3 toasts and has no history.
- **Placement.** Bottom-right, styled like `day-session-bar`'s existing undo toast so they read as one family. Not rendered into the PiP.
- **Wired to:**
  - **I1:** "Couldn't save this session — Retry". Today one `try` block covers three writes (`:680-687`): `savePomodoro`, `incrementTaskCompletedPomodoros` and `syncSlotAutoStatus`. Re-running all three after the save succeeded and the increment failed would credit the task twice.
    - So `persistWorkSession` records which step failed, and Retry resumes **from that step**.
    - Only `savePomodoro` is idempotent (its doc id is the `runId`). The increment is not.
    - A failed rating save gets "Couldn't save your rating".
  - **I2:** "Logged 18 min to CS201 Revision". Shown by the tab that ran `startFocus`.
  - **I3:** "CS201 Revision — closed". Shown by the tab that logged the session, plus S5 when it is on.
- **Not in this doc.** Moving the two existing undo toasts onto the host is a follow-up that touches no behaviour (§6, D4).

### 5.5 Settings UI

The Reminders card (`settings/page.tsx:96-128`) becomes **"Reminders, sounds & notifications"**. The copy at `:119` changes to drop "this tab", once F2 and F4 are fixed.

- **Sounds:**
  - master toggle;
  - volume slider;
  - one checkbox per cue, each with a ▶ *Test* button (the button also unlocks audio);
  - the ambient toggle, if Phase 5 ships.
- **Notifications:**
  - "Tell me when a focus session or break ends" (N1);
  - "Announce each block as it starts" (N3);
  - the existing sitting lead and end row.
  - All three share the existing permission explainer (`:328-379`), which moves up so it covers the whole group.

## 7. Acceptance criteria

**Settled by a pure `scripts/verify-notify.ts`** (no DOM; `claimCue` is injected with a fake store):

1. `reminderKey` and `leadKey` include the date. The same slot id on two dates gives two keys.
2. `claimCue(key)` returns true exactly once per key across two simulated tabs, and true again after the TTL expires.
3. Cue resolution: `sounds` absent means `sessionEnd` and `breakEnd` on and the rest off; `enabled:false` means everything off; `dayOff` means S3–S5 off and S1 and S2 unaffected.

**Only provable in the running app** (Chrome, test account `suyashbima@`; method from the 2026-10-14 timer-testing notes):

4. **F1 fixed.** Leave a hydration reminder unanswered for 3 minutes: there is **one** OS notification, not six.
5. **F2 fixed.** With two FocusOS tabs open, every reminder, block start and sitting lead/end produces exactly one notification and one sound. Pressing Skip in tab A clears the banner in tab B.
6. **F3 fixed.** A `meal` block starting raises nothing. A deep-work block raises "X starting." unless the toggle is off.
7. **F4 fixed.** With `/read` as the only tab, a hydration reminder and a sitting lead notification both arrive.
8. **S1.** A focus countdown ending produces one chime:
   - **Start pressed in the PiP after a cold reload, with no click in the main tab.** Test this first, because it settles C1's open question.
   - with one tab;
   - with two tabs;
   - with two tabs where the background one was reloaded and never clicked: the chime comes from the clicked tab, never silence;
   - with the PiP open;
   - with the PiP closed and the tab hidden for more than 5 minutes. **Record the measured lag; this is the input to the §5.2 scheduling decision.**
9. **S1/S2 negatives.** No chime for *Finish now* (any length, work or break), for *Skip break*, for *Reset*, or for an auto-log on switch (I2).
10. **S2.** A break that runs out chimes once.
11. **C1.** After a cold reload and no click, a reminder firing 2 minutes later plays no in-app sound, still raises its OS notification, and throws no console error.
12. **N1.** With `timerEnd` on and the tab focused, there is no OS notification. With the tab hidden there is exactly one, and clicking it focuses the tab.
13. **I1.** Going offline in DevTools does **not** test this: Firestore queues writes offline instead of rejecting them, which is why `use-day-autosave.ts` checks `navigator.onLine` itself. Inject the failure instead, using a dev-only throw flag at each of the three steps in turn. In each case:
    - the error toast appears;
    - Retry writes exactly one session doc;
    - the task's pomodoro count goes up by exactly one.
14. **Mute.** `sounds.enabled:false` silences every cue in every tab without a reload. Settings arrive through `subscribeUserSettings` (`hooks/use-user-settings.ts:21`), a live subscription.

## 8. Phases and estimate

Estimates are in focused working hours, including verification in the running app. Most of the time goes to verification, not code: multi-tab, PiP, hidden-tab and permission states each need a real run, and the hidden-tab check alone takes more than 5 minutes per attempt.

| **4. Toasts** | `toast-host.tsx`, I1 with Retry, I2, I3 and S5 | 3 h | 2 h | **~5 h** |
| **Core total** | | | | **~26 h ≈ 4 working days** (~29 h if §5.2 scheduling is needed) |

**Order matters.** Phase 0 must ship before any sound is attached to reminders: on today's code, S4 would beep every 30 seconds (F1) in every open tab (F2). Phases 1 and 2 deliver the main user-visible win (the timer chimes) and can ship on their own. Phases 3 and 4 are independent of each other.

**Risks that could move the estimate:**

- **C2.** If Chrome's lag is large and audio-clock scheduling is needed, add +3 h.
- **Hoisting the provider (Phase 3).** It could disturb the reader's render or the admin pages. Fallback is option (b) in §5.3, +2 h.
- **Notification delivery.** macOS Focus / Do Not Disturb silently swallows notifications. That only affects verification, not the build, but it can cost time while testing.

## 9. As built (2026-10-27)

### 9.1 What changed from the spec

- **Real recordings, not synthesis.** Partway through the build the owner asked for downloaded open-source sounds instead of sounds generated in code. `lib/sounds.ts` therefore plays files from `public/sounds/` through plain `<audio>` elements. There is no Web Audio at all. Each file ships as `.ogg`, plus an AAC `.m4a` copy for Safari; the browser picks one with `canPlayType`. Sources and licences are in `public/sounds/CREDITS.md`.

  | Cue | File | Source |
  |---|---|---|
  | S1 session end | `session-end` | Kenney *Music Jingles*, `jingles_STEEL02` (steel drum, 1.4 s) |
  | S2 break end | `break-end` | Kenney *Music Jingles*, `jingles_PIZZI02` (pizzicato, 1 s) |
  | S3 heads-up | `heads-up` | Kenney *Interface Sounds*, `glass_001` |
  | S4 reminder | `reminder` | Kenney *Interface Sounds*, `drop_003` |
  | S5 block closed | `block-closed` | Kenney *Interface Sounds*, `confirmation_001` |
  | Ambient | `noise-brown`, `noise-grey`, `noise-white` | Wikimedia Commons, all public domain |

  - The Kenney packs are CC0. The cues were chosen by measuring each file, not by listening: rising pitch for an ending, short and quiet for the minor cues. Swap any of them by replacing the file of the same name.
  - **Grey noise replaces pink.** The only pink-noise file on Commons is CC-BY-SA, so it was skipped. Grey noise is the perceptually even option instead. It is recorded about 3× louder than the other two, so `NOISE_FILES` gives it a 0.33 gain.
- **Readiness (C1).** Whether a tab can play is now `navigator.userActivation.hasBeenActive`, falling back to a flag set on the first gesture. The `AudioContext` state is gone.
- **The PiP question is moot.** Chrome only opens the floating widget from a click in the main tab (`requestWindow` needs one), and that click already unlocks audio there. So "Start pressed in the PiP with no click in the main tab" can't happen. Clicks inside the PiP are still forwarded to `unlockAudio` (`floating-widget-button.tsx`).
- **No audio-clock scheduling (§5.2).** The cue fires when the timer notices zero. C2 was not measured (§9.3). With the floating widget open, or background noise on, the tick isn't throttled, so the chime is on time.
- **The noise plays from one tab** (`hooks/use-ambient-sound.ts`). The `focusos-ambient` Web Lock elects it: other tabs queue behind it and take over if it closes. A tab only joins once it may play audio.
- **Toasts** (`components/toast-host.tsx`) can be broadcast over a `BroadcastChannel`. A Retry pressed in any tab runs in the tab that raised the toast.
- **Retry resumes from the failed step.** `persistSteps` runs save → task credit → block status in order, and retries from whichever step failed.

## 10. Background-sound mixer (added 2026-10-27, after §9)

**The ask:** "i want rain, fire, cafe etc, want a mixer at top to do it." Then: "you can replace the cross button with play pause in floating widget."

**Owner's choices:**
- Playback works both ways: a play/pause button, and "also play while a focus session runs".
- Public-domain and CC-BY recordings only, with credit.
- All four sound sets.
- The control lives in the top bar and the floating widget.

**What was built:**
- **Mixer UI.** `components/soundscape-mixer.tsx`:
  - The headphones button in the app's top bar and the reader's toolbar opens a panel.
  - The panel has play/pause, a master volume, "also play while a focus session runs", and a slider per sound in four groups: Weather, Nature, Places, Noise.
  - Tapping a sound's name toggles it between off and 50%.
  - "Turn all off" clears the mix, and a "Sound credits" list sits at the bottom.
- **Floating widget.** The widget's corner × is now the mix's play/pause. The PiP window keeps its own browser close button.
- **Sounds.** 11 loops in `public/sounds/scape/*.m4a` plus the three existing noise files, about 8 MB in total. Each file is only fetched once its slider is up.
  - Each loop was cut from a Commons recording, cross-faded into a seamless loop and level-matched to about −26 dBFS.
  - Fire is soft-limited. Keyboard is deliberately quieter.
  - Sources, authors and licences are in `lib/soundscape.ts` (`credit`) and `public/sounds/CREDITS.md`.
- **State.** `components/soundscape-provider.tsx`, mounted at the root inside the focus timer:
  - The mix (levels, master, autoFocus) is a synced setting, `UserSettings.soundscape`. Slider drags apply immediately and are written to Firestore 600 ms after the drag settles.
  - Play/pause is per device (`focusos-soundscape` in localStorage), shared by that device's tabs.
  - Pausing an auto-playing mix during a focus countdown keeps it quiet for that countdown only (`mutedRunId`).
  - One tab plays, chosen by the `focusos-soundscape` Web Lock.
- **Replaced from Phase 5.** The single "background noise" switch and the timer card's wave-icon toggle are gone. An account that had noise switched on gets that noise as its mix (`resolveSoundscape`). Settings now shows only a pointer to the mixer and the auto-play switch. Its "Sounds" switch, renamed "Alert sounds", covers the alert sounds only.

**Verified in Chrome on the test account:**
- The panel renders.
- Rain, fire and café played together at their own levels, looping, at the right lengths.
- A slider drag changed the volume live (fire 0.5 → 0.26), and the levels were back after a reload, read from Firestore.
- The widget's corner button paused and resumed the mix.
- Starting a focus session auto-played the mix. Pausing it during that session kept it quiet while the timer ran, and `mutedRunId` was set.
- With two tabs, one held the lock and the other queued, silent.
- The reader toolbar has the button.

**Not verified:**
- Nobody has listened to the loops. They were chosen and trimmed by measuring loudness, not by ear.
- Whether there's an audible gap where each AAC loop repeats. The encoder writes the AAC start padding into the file so browsers can trim it, but whether Chrome actually does was not checked.
- Safari.
